Understanding Amendment 3

A closer look at what could change.

Amendment 3 appears on the November 3, 2026 statewide ballot. If approved, it changes how homestead property is taxed for non-school services.

A larger exemption, phased in over time

Today's exemption is up to approximately $51,411. The proposal increases the non-school portion and lowers the assessment cap for non-homestead property.

Why it matters locally Police, fire, streets, parks, planning, and city operations rely on non-school property taxes.
  1. 01
    $25,000 school-tax exemption

    The school-tax portion remains capped at $25,000.

  2. 02
    Up to $150,000 in 2027

    Non-school homestead exemption in the first year.

  3. 03
    Up to $250,000 in 2028

    Annual inflation adjustments would begin in 2029.

  4. 04
    Assessment cap reduced to 5%

    For non-school assessments of non-homestead property, down from 10%. This caps assessed-value growth, not tax rates.

  5. 05
    A process for future exemption increases

    The Legislature would establish a process for counties and cities to increase exemptions further. The proposal does not set a date for full elimination.

Eligibility depends on homestead status and Florida residency. New residents arriving in 2027 or later follow a different phase-in. Read the Volusia County Property Appraiser’s Amendment 3 FAQ for details.

What Could Amendment 3 Mean for Your Household?

This calculator shows how the proposed exemption change could affect the City portion of your property tax bill if your home qualifies. It uses a fixed assessed value and DeLand's current 6.1841-mill rate. At a $300,000 assessed value, the estimated annual savings are about $610 in 2027 and $1,228 in 2028. Your actual bill will depend on your eligibility, any other exemptions you receive, what your assessed value is in future years, and what tax rates the City adopts.

    A City Budget: Built By You

    Which trade-offs would you choose? Close the anticipated FY 27/28 funding gap by selecting service cuts, potential revenue, or both. You can submit when their combined annual impact closes the full $5.2 million deficit. This would only account for year one (FY 27/28) services changes. Additional cuts or fees would be necessary to reach the full $8.8 million gap in year two.

    A few things to note: All services listed include personnel, materials, and operational costs. Additional necessary costs, such as Human Resources and IT support, have already been factored in for ease of use. The City's General Fund for FY 26/27 is $54 million. Part of that money is already committed to required costs like transfers, contingencies, and shared expenses. The remaining $42 million is spread across five departments. In this exercise, you’ll get to decide where you'd be comfortable cutting services to adjust to the proposed amendment. This exercise only includes departments and divisions funded by the City’s General Fund. Services funded by fees or assessments (such as Utilities and Stormwater) would not be impacted by Amendment 3, excluding them from the challenge. Figures use FY 26/27 net calculations, which already account for existing revenue streams.
